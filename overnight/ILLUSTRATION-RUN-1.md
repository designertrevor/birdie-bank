## Where we landed

The family is a single confident brush ink line, flat fills, lots of white, calm faces, bodies that differ a lot from one golfer to the next. It came from your farmer's-market reference, not the cut-paper car. Two Recraft outputs of the first tee, N and R, are attached as the style references in the project and every drawing after them was made with those two on. That is what kept the cast and the line the same across eleven subjects.

Three things the references carry that we don't want, and that I'd fix in the files rather than by re-rolling:

1. **Lime.** N and R have a lime ground. Every run after them pulled lime in somewhere, even with our palette on and the color named in the prompt: the wallet, the trophy cup, the ball faces, the buddies, the birdie, the ball on the tee. The palette feature holds on clothes and fails on the thing the reference is most sure about. The fix is a color snap on export, with the ground and the ball set by hand.
2. **Frowns.** About one face per scene comes out frowning, and the second buddies sheet frowns on all three. The references frown, so the style does. Mouth paths in the SVG are small and easy to flatten.
3. **Cream ball is not cream.** The ball keeps coming out lime, except on the sleeping ball where it came out right. Same fix as 1.

## What this tells us about the family

- **The tier split works.** Scenes, spots, stickers, avatars and critters all came from the same two references and look like one hand drew them. The small tier is just the big tier's line with fewer fills, which is what the brief wanted.
- **Faces are the one thing to standardize by hand.** Dot eyes, a nose, and either nothing or a flat line. The model will not hold "no frown" from a reference that frowns, so the mouth gets fixed in the file every time, and the two frown-free references (buddies-1, sleep-1) should replace N and R as the face reference once they're exported.
- **Color is a file job, not a prompt job.** After 20 runs the pattern is fixed: palette on, hex in the prompt, and the reference's lime still wins. A snap script with per-drawing overrides (ground to mint, ball to cream) takes it off the table.
- **Dark mode needs its own line color.** Black ink vanishes on #1a1f1e wherever the fill is also dark.
